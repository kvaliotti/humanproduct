# Writing rules

Two short lists. The prose reviewer flags these when they cost the reader something. Every sentence this plugin writes (findings, fixes, the merged review) follows them.

## Clutter and clarity (after Zinsser, *On Writing Well*)

- **Cut words that do no work.** "In order to" → "to". "At this point in time" → "now". "Very", "really", "quite", "basically", and "actually" rarely add anything. Neither do pairs that say one thing twice ("each and every", "first and foremost").
- **Plain word over fancy word.** "Use", not "utilize". "Help", not "facilitate". "Start", not "commence".
- **Specific over abstract.** "Signups fell 12% in May", not "engagement metrics experienced a decline". Turn nouns back into verbs: "we decided", not "we made a decision".
- **Say who does what.** Passive voice is fine until it hides an actor the reader needs.
- **Commit or cut.** Stacked hedges ("may potentially", "somewhat", "it could be argued") tell the reader the writer doesn't believe it.
- **Stop when you're done.** No closing paragraph that restates the piece.

## AI-writing tells

Readers spot these as machine text and start skimming, so they cost credibility as well as words. Flag them when they stand in for a specific point.

- **Throat-clearing.** "In today's fast-paced world", "It's important to note that", "Let's dive in", "When it comes to".
- **Reflexive contrast.** "It's not X, it's Y." "Not just X, but Y." Fine once; a tic when it frames every point.
- **Staged reveals.** "The answer? Simple." "Here's the thing:" "The result:" A colon or a question doing a sentence's job.
- **Triplets by habit.** Three adjectives or three parallel clauses where one specific item would do.
- **Inflated words.** delve, leverage, robust, seamless, crucial, pivotal, landscape, realm, tapestry, unlock, empower, elevate, foster, harness, underscore, testament, navigate (for anything but a route). Each has a plainer word, or needs none.
- **Hollow intensifiers.** "truly", "incredibly", "deeply", "game-changing", "cutting-edge".
- **Trailing -ing clauses.** "…, highlighting the need for…", "…, ensuring that…", "…, making it easier to…". They claim a significance the sentence hasn't earned.
- **Dash chains.** An em dash in most sentences, standing in for commas, colons, and full stops.
- **Formatting instead of thought.** Bullets of fragments where the argument needs sentences; bold on every other phrase; headers on a one-page piece.
- **Neat endings.** "Ultimately, …", "In conclusion, …", a closing line about the journey or the future.
- **Chatbot register.** "Great question", "I hope this helps", "Certainly!"

One instance is rarely a finding. A repeating pattern is one finding with up to 3 quotes and a count. It is Major when it runs through the whole piece, because the reader will discount everything else.
