# Lens: Cognitive Ease & Decision Design

**Source:** *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (Kahneman). Only the decision-and-perception contribution. Loop mechanics belong to behavioural-loop and are not re-rated here.

Users decide fast and avoid effort. At every choice point the product either makes the valuable action the one a distracted user takes without thinking, or loses them to friction, ambiguity, a competing option, or a bias pointed the wrong way. The question: **is the valuable path also the fluent, obvious, low-risk, well-framed path?**

You surface bias-based mechanisms (framing, loss aversion, scarcity, social proof). Flag whether honest or manipulative use is at stake; the ethics call is made by the control-autonomy lens and the ethics gate.

## 1. The decision point *(behaviour)*

- Is the desired action obvious without searching, and dominant over competing actions? Is the default path the desired path?
- Recognition over recall: does anything need to be held in memory?
- Familiar patterns where novelty would only add effort?
- Can a distracted, tired user complete it?

## 2. Fluency *(behaviour, adoption)*

- Is the language clear rather than clever, with no jargon at decision points?
- Is there strain near key actions (clutter, dense text, tiny targets, unclear icons)? Strain quietly pushes users toward "no."
- Can the user predict what happens after clicking, and tell afterwards that it worked (saved, sent, undoable)?
- Does the clearest path lead somewhere valuable, or somewhere shallow?

## 3. Effort budget *(behaviour, adoption)*

- Are decisions asked for only after value is clear? Progressive disclosure instead of front-loaded options?
- Are repeated decisions automated, and do defaults or examples decide where the user has no basis to choose?
- Is friction kept for moments where reflection helps (destructive, irreversible actions) and nowhere else?
- Can users recover from a small error without restarting the flow?

## 4. Framing *(behaviour, adoption, engagement)*

- Is onboarding framed as progress toward value, not setup work? Upgrade as unlocking, not a tax? Reactivation as restarting progress, not correcting failure?
- Is repeated use framed as accumulation?
- Does framing clarify reality rather than distort it?

## 5. Anchoring *(adoption, engagement)*

- Is the first number, example, or benchmark the user sees chosen to anchor toward value? Value before price, outcomes before features?
- Accidental low anchors: an empty state that anchors inactivity, a manual first run when automation is the goal, a shallow first use when depth is the goal.

## 6. Defaults and choice architecture *(behaviour, adoption)*

- Does each default serve the user's likely best interest and the target behaviour, while staying visible and easy to change?
- Presets where users lack expertise, no customisation before value is felt?
- Confirmations only where mistakes matter, not dialogs users learn to click through?

## 7. Loss aversion and status quo *(adoption)*

- What do users fear losing by switching? Is it reduced (map old to new, reversible trial, safe first step)?
- Is the cost of the status quo made visible without guilt?

## 8. Deciding on the right question *(behaviour)*

- **What you see is all there is:** is a key tradeoff hidden behind a persuasive CTA? Do users correctly understand the offer, commitment, and risk?
- **Substitution:** is the user answering an easier question ("Is this pretty?" instead of "Will this help me?")?
- Does the **team** substitute too? NPS for adoption, time-on-page for engagement, clicks for task completion. Flag where measurement is fooling itself.

## 9. Salience and memory *(engagement)*

- Is the most important element the most salient, with salience guiding rather than decorating?
- **Peak-end:** does the experience have a clear progress peak and end well? One bad final moment taints the memory and the decision to return. Are onboarding, purchase, support, and cancellation endings designed?
- Are the worst moments (waiting, uncertainty, repetitive effort) improved, not just the average?
